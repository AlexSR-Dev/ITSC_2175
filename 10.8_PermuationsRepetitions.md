3. Formula For Counting Permutations with Repetition

n! / n_1!n_2!...n_k!
• n! counts the arrangements if every object were distinct.
• Each n_i! accounts for the repeated objects.
• Dividing by the product of these factorials removes the duplicate arrangements created by exchanging identical objects.

- Use when arranging a collection contains repeated elements.

4. Participation Activity 10.8.2 - Examples of Counting Subsets

Q1) PEPPER
ASKS: How many ways are there to permute the letters in PEPPER?
STEP 1 - Identify the total number of letters & repetitions:
• P appears 3 times.
• E appears 2 times.
• R appears 1 time.

TOTAL: n = 6

STEP 2 - Apply Formula
6! / 3!2!1! = 6 * 5 * 4 / 2 * 1 = 60
RESULT: 60, distinct arrangements.



Q2) HAPPY

STEP 1 - Identify total & repetitions:
• P appears 2 times.
• H, A, and Y appear once.

TOTAL = 5

STEP 2 - Apply Formula
5! / 2!1!1!1! = 5 * 4 * 3 / 1 = 60
RESULT: 60 distinct arrangements.

5. Example 10.8.1 - Countuing Assignments Using Permutations with Repetition
CONSIDER: A company with 11 employees that rents an office space with three offies:
GIVEN:
• Office A with 3 people.
• Office B with 4 people.
• Office C with 4 people.
• Total people 11.

ASKS: How many different assignments are there for employees to officies?


METHOD 1 - Using permutations with repetition.
SET: S = {AAABBBBCCCC}
• 3 A's for office A
• 4 B's for office B
• 4 C's for office C

11! / 3!4!4! = 11,550


METHOD 2 - Using the Generalized Product Rule
STEP 1 - Select 3 employees for Office A.
- 11 employees available:
(11 3) = 11! / 3!(11-3)! = 11! / 3!8! = 11 * 10 * 9/ 3 * 2 * 1 = 165

STEP 2 - Select 4 employees for Office B.
- 8 employees remain:
(8 4) = 70

STEP 3 - Select 4 employees for Office C.
- 4 employees remain:
(4 4) = 1

STEP 4 - Apply the product rule.
(11 3) (8 4) (4 4) 
= 165 * 70 * 1
= 11,550
