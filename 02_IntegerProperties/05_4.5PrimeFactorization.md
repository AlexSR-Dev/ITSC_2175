05 - 4.5 Prime Factorization


1. Prime Factorization
- A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.
- Expresses a number as a product of prime numbers.

General Steps:
To find the prime factorization of a number:
1) Find a prime number that divides the number.
2) Divide.
3) Continue factoring the quotient.
4) Stop when all factors are prime.
5) Write the prime factors in increasing order.
6) Combine repeated primes using exponents.

Example: 120 = 2 * 2 * 2 * 3 * 5
Since 2 occurs three times: 120 = 2^3 * 3 * 5

IMPORTANT:
- Doe not include 1 in a prime factorization.
- The factors must ultimately all be prime.
- Repeated primes should be represented with exponents.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. 4.5.1 - Identifying Prime Factorizations
- Determine which expression is actually the prime factorization of a number.

General Method:
1) Multiply the factors to see which expression equals the target number.
2) Check that every factor is prime.
3) Check that the primes are written in increasing/nondecreasing order if required.
4) Select the expression satisfying all condition

Example pattern
For 48: 48 = 2^4 * 3
Check:
- 2 is prime
- 3 is prime
- 2 < 3
- Product equals 48
Thus, this is a valid prime factorization.

1) Which of the following is the prime factorization for 48?
2^4 * 3

2) What is the prime factorization for 31?
31, is already a prime number, thus the factorization is itself.

3) In which prime factorization are the prime factors listed in nondecreasing order?
2 * 7 * 11 * 13

3. Ordering Prime Factors
Prime factors should be listed in nondecreasing order: 2,3,5,7,11,...
- Each factor must be equal to or greater than the previous factor.

General Method:
1) Factor the number completely.
2) List the prime factors from smallest to largest.
3) Combine repeated factors with exponents.

EX: The factors are: 2 * 7 * 11 * 13
Correctly ordered because: 2 < 7 < 11 < 13
BUT: 2 * 7 * 13 * 11, is not in nondecreasing order.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. Finding GCD from Prime Factorizations
- The greates common divisor (GCD) is the largest positive integer that divides both numbers.
When the prime factorizations are given, use the smallest exponent of each common prime.

General Steps:
Given two prime factorizations:
1. Write both numbers using the same set of primes.
2. Look for primes appearing in both numbers.
3. For each common prime, choose the smaller exponent.
4. Multiply those selected prime powers.
5. The result is the GCD.

EX:
24 = 2^3 *3
50 = 2 * 5 ^ 2

Common prime: 2
Compare exponents: 2^3 and 2^1
Choose the smaller: 2^1
Thus: gcd(24,50) = 2

GCD shortcut: GCD = Common primes with the smallest exponents.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. Finding LCM from Prime Factorizations
- The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of both numbers.
For prime factorization, use the largest exponent of every prime appearing in either number.

General Steps:
1) Write both prime factorizations using the same set of primes.
2) List every prime that appears.
3) For each prime, choose the larger exponent.
4) Multiply the resulting prime powers.
5) The product is the LCM.

EX:
24 = 2^3 * 3
50 = 2 * 5^2

Use:
2^3 - Larger exponent of 2
3^1 - Only appears in 24
5^2 - Only appears in 50

THUS: LCM(24,50) = 2^3 * 3 * 5^2 = 600

LCM Shortcut: LCM = All primes with the largest exponents.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6. Common-Prime Set
- When finding GCD or LCM, it can help to create a common set of primes first.

EX:
24 = 2^3 * 3
50 = 2 * 5^2

Common prime set: 2, 3, 5
Rewrite both:
24 = 2^3 * 3^1 * 5^0
50 = 2^1 * 3^0 * 5^2

Compare the exponents:
| Prime | 24 | 50 | GCD → smaller | LCM → larger |
| ----- | -: | -: | ------------: | -----------: |
| 2     |  3 |  1 |             1 |            3 |
| 3     |  1 |  0 |             0 |            1 |
| 5     |  0 |  2 |             0 |            2 |

7. 4.5.2 - GCD & LCM
- Provides prime factorizations and find the expression for the GCD and LCM

Use this process
For GCD: Compare exponents -> Choose smaller -> multiply
For LCM: Compare exponents -> Choose larger -> multiply

924 = 2^2 * 3^1 * 5^0 * 7^1 * 11^1
33075 = 2^0 * 3^3 * 5^2 * 7^2 * 11^0

LCM(924, 33075) = 2^2 * 3^3 * 5^2 * 7^2 * 11^1 = 1,455,300
- Keep the largest exponent, example for 2 theres an exponent of 2 for 924 and 0 for 33075, thus keep 2.
GCD(924, 33075) = 2^0 * 3^1 * 5^0 * 7^1 * 11^0 = 21
- Keep the smallest exponent.

8. 4.5.3 - Matching GCD & LCM expressions
Given:
147 = 3 * 7^2
315 = 3^2 * 5 * 7
550 = 2 * 5^2 * 11

General Strategy
Step 1 -  Identify the two numbers
EX: GCD(147,315)

Step 2 - List their prime factors:
147 = 3^1 * 7^2
315 = 3^2 * 5^1 * 7^1

Step 3 - Compare exponents:
For GCD, choose the smaller:
3^1 * 7^1 == 3 * 7

Step 4 - For LCM, choose the larger
3^2 * 5^1 * 7^2


1) 2 * 3^2 * 5^2 * 7 * 11
- Here we have every unique value, BUT each one has the LARGEST exponent.
LCM(315, 550)

2) 21
- Definitely the result of a LCM or GCD
147 = 3^1 * 5^0 * 7^2
315 = 3^2 * 5^1 * 7^1
GCD(147, 315) = 3 * 1 * 7 = 21

3) 5
315 = 2^0 * 3^2 * 5^1 * 7^1 * 11^0
550 = 2^1 * 3^0 * 5^2 * 7^0 * 11^1
GCD(550,315) = 1 * 1 * 5 * 1 * 1 = 5

4) 3^2 * 5 * 7^2
LCM(147, 315)

9. Challenge Activity 4.5.1 - Prime Factorizations

General solving process

For any number:
Step 1: Start dividing by the smallest prime possible.
Usually begin with: 2 -> 3 -> 5 -> 7 -> 11 -> ...

Step 2: Continue dividing while the prime divides evenly.
Step 3: Move to the next prime when it no longer divides evenly.
Step 4: Stop once the remaining value is prime.
Step 5: Count repeated factors.
Step 6: Convert repeated factors into exponents.
Step 7: Write primes in increasing order.

80

= 2 * 2 * 20
= 2 * 2 * 2 * 10
