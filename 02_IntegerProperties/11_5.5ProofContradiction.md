11 - 5.5 Proof By Contradiction

1. Goals
: Understand the logical structure of a proof by contradiction.
: Express the negation of a theorem.
: Distinguish between:
    - Direct proof
    - Proof by contrapositive
    - Proof by contradiction
: Write contradition proofs for different types of theorems.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Proof By Contradiction
- Starts by assuming that the theorem is false.
Then you can use that assumption to logically derive something that cannot be true.

Structure:
Assume the theorem is false
->
Follow the logical steps
->
Reach a contradiction
->
Therefore, the original theorem must be true

- Called an indirect proof.

IDEA:
If assuming: "The theorem is false" forces you to reach something impossible, then your assumption must have been false.
Thus, the original theorem is true.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. How to recognize a contradiction proof
Look for languages such as:
: "Suppose the theorem is false..."
: "Assume, for contradiction..."
: "Assume the negation..."
: "This contradicts..."
: "This is inconsistent with..."

Important Question to ask: "What would the negation of this theorem say?"

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. The logical structure
- If the theorem is represented by T:

Direct Proof: T, is established directly.

Contradiction proof:
Starts with: ¬T
Then derive something impossible.
EX: ¬T -> R ∧ ¬R
- Since R and -R cannot both be true, the assumption -T must be false.
THUS: T

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. 5.5.1 - Proof by Contradiction
- "For every pair of positive real numbers a and b,"
sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) != sqrt(a + b)

1) Step 1 - Assume the theorem is false
- The theorem says the two expression are not equal.
- So its negation says they are equal.

Assume: a > 0, b > 0
AND: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) = sqrt(a + b)

Recognition Trick:
- When the theorem says: "Expression A != Expression B"
Contradiction assumption becomes:
- "Expression A = Expression B."




2) Step 2 - Simplify the assumed equation
- Square both sides: (sqrt(a) + sqrt(b))^2 = (sqrt(a + b))^2
- Expand the parentheses
- Giving: a + 2(sqrt(ab)) + b = a + b

- Subtract a and b on both sides: 2(sqrt(ab)) = 0
- Divide by 2: ab = 0
THUS: a = 0 or b = 0.




3) Identify the contradiction
- Our original assumption: a > 0, b > 0.
So a and b cannot be zero.

Thus, we reached a contradiction.

Answer: The assumption that: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) = sqrt(a + b), must be false.
THUS: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) != sqrt(a + b)
for positive a, b.
