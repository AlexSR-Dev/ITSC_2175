11 - 5.5 Proof By Contradiction

1. Goals
: Understand the logical structure of a proof by contradiction.
: Express the negation of a theorem.
: Distinguish between:
    - Direct proof
    - Proof by contrapositive
    - Proof by contradiction
: Write contradition proofs for different types of theorems.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2. Proof By Contradiction
- Starts by assuming that the theorem is false.
Then you can use that assumption to logically derive something that cannot be true.

Structure:
Assume the theorem is false
->
Follow the logical steps
->
Reach a contradiction
->
Therefore, the original theorem must be true

- Called an indirect proof.

IDEA:
If assuming: "The theorem is false" forces you to reach something impossible, then your assumption must have been false.
Thus, the original theorem is true.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3. How to recognize a contradiction proof
Look for languages such as:
: "Suppose the theorem is false..."
: "Assume, for contradiction..."
: "Assume the negation..."
: "This contradicts..."
: "This is inconsistent with..."

Important Question to ask: "What would the negation of this theorem say?"

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4. The logical structure
- If the theorem is represented by T:

Direct Proof: T, is established directly.

Contradiction proof:
Starts with: ¬T
Then derive something impossible.
EX: ¬T -> R ∧ ¬R
- Since R and -R cannot both be true, the assumption -T must be false.
THUS: T

- You assume R is true and -R is false at the same time.

5. 5.5.1 - Proof by Contradiction
- "For every pair of positive real numbers a and b,"
sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) != sqrt(a + b)

1) Step 1 - Assume the theorem is false
- The theorem says the two expression are not equal.
- So its negation says they are equal.

Assume: a > 0, b > 0
AND: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) = sqrt(a + b)

Recognition Trick:
- When the theorem says: "Expression A != Expression B"
Contradiction assumption becomes:
- "Expression A = Expression B."




2) Step 2 - Simplify the assumed equation
- Square both sides: (sqrt(a) + sqrt(b))^2 = (sqrt(a + b))^2
- Expand the parentheses
- Giving: a + 2(sqrt(ab)) + b = a + b

- Subtract a and b on both sides: 2(sqrt(ab)) = 0
- Divide by 2: ab = 0
THUS: a = 0 or b = 0.




3) Identify the contradiction
- Our original assumption: a > 0, b > 0.
So a and b cannot be zero.

Thus, we reached a contradiction.

Answer: The assumption that: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) = sqrt(a + b), must be false.
THUS: sqrt(a) + sqrt(b) != sqrt(a + b)
for positive a, b.

6. 5.5.2 - Expression the Negation of Theorems
- Before writing a contradiction proof, you need to know exactly what the theorem's negation says.

1) "Among any pair of consecutive integers, at least one must be even."
Negation: There exists a pair of consecutive integers that are both odd.

WHY?
- The original says: "Every pair has at least one even."
- To make it false: "At least one pair has no even integers."
THUS, the reason for the negation.


2) "The negative of an irrational number is irrational."
Negation: "There exists an irrational number s such that -s is rational."

WHY?
- The original says: "Every irrational s produces an irrational -s."
- Thus, to make it false, find one irrational whoe negative is not irrational.


3) "There exists a pair of integers x, y such that 6x - 21y = 1."
Negation: "For every pair of integers x,y."
6x - 21y != 1.

Recognition Rule:
- Watch the quantifiers:
: "There exists" -> Negation becomes for every.
: "For every" -> Negation becomes there exists.

AND:
: = -> !=
: > -> <=
: < -> >=

7. Contradiction vs. Contrapositive

Direct Proof
For: p -> q
You assume: p
and prove: q
Pattern: p -> q


Proof by contrapositive
Assume: -q
and prove: -p
Pattern: -q -> -p


Proof by contradiction
- For a conditional theorem, you assume the negation of the entire statement:
-(p -> q), which is equivalent to: p ∧ ¬q

- Then derive some contradiction
Pattern: p ∧ ¬q -> contradiction

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8. 5.3.3 - Matching Proof Types
Theorem: p -> q

1) "Assume p. Follow steps to conclude q."
- Direct proof.

2) "Assume -q. Follow steps to conclude -p."
- Proof by contrapositive.

3) "Assume p ∧ ¬q. Follow logical steps to conclude r ∧ ¬r."
- Proof by contradiction

Shortcut: Look at the first assumption.

9. 5.5.4 - Contradiction of Conditional Statements

Q1) Theorem: "For every integer n, if n^2 is odd, then n is odd."
Structure: p -> q
p: n^2 is odd
q: n is odd

- To contradict the theorem assume:
p ∧ ¬q

So: n^2 is odd
AND: n is even.

RESULT: "There exists an ineger n such that n^2 is odd and n is even."



Q2) Theorem: "The product of a nonzero rational number and a nonzero irrational number is irrational."
r = rational,
s = irrational.
Implies: rs = irrational

- To contradict it:
r = rational
s = irrational
rs = rational

RESULT: "There are nonzero r, s such that r is rational, s is irrational, and rs is rational."
RECOGNIZE: If p, then q.

10. Classic Proof by Contradiction: sqrt(2) is irrational

Theorem: sqrt(2) is irrational

Step 1 - Assume the opposite
Assume: sqrt(2) is rational.

- Since its rational, we write: sqrt(2) = n / d
: n, d are integers,
: d != 0,
: n and d have no common factor greater than 1.
- Meaning the fraction is in lowest terms.


Step 2 - Square
sqrt(2) = n / d

- Square both sides:
2 = n^2 / d^2

- Multiply by d^2:
2d^2 - n^2.


Step 3 - Show n is even
Since: n^2 = 2d^2
- n^2 is even.
As: "If the square of an integer is even, then the integer itself is even."

THUS: n is even.
Writes: n = 2k, for some integer k.


Step 4 - Substitute
n^2 = (2k)^2 = 4k^2

Since: 2d^2 = n^2
THUS: 2d^2 = 4k^2

Divide by 2: d^2 = 2k^2
So d^2 is even.
THUS: d is even.


Step 5 - Find the contradiction
We established:
: n is even
AND
: d is even
- Thus, both are divisible by 2.

- HOWEVER, we original assumed n/d had no common factor greater than 1.
Impossible.
Contradiction!
Thus the original assumption: sqrt(2) is rational must be false.

RESULT: sqrt(2) is irrational.

11. 5.5.5 - Ordering the srqt(2) Proof
Logical Sequence:

1) Assume: sqrt(2) is rational.

2) Thus: sqrt(2) = n / d
Where n,d are integers, d != 0, and they have no common factor greater than 1.
- "Therefore, sqrt(2) can be expressed as n/d, where n and  d are integers, d != 0, and no integer greater than 1 that
divides both n and d."

3) Square and multiple by d^2: 2d^2 = n^2
- "Square both sides of the equation sqrt(2) = n/d and multiply by d^2 to get: n^2 = 2d^2"

4) Since n^2 = 2d^2, n^2 is even.
- "Since n^2 = 2d^2 is equal to 2 times an integer, n^2 is even and therefore n is even."

5) Thus n is even, so: n = 2k
- "Since n is even, n = 2k for some integer k. We can substitute 2k for n in the equation n^2 = 2d^2 to get 4k^2 = 2d^2."

6) Substitute: n^2 = (2k)^2 = 4k^2

7) Combine with 2d^2 = n^2:
2d^2 = 4k^2
so:
d^2 = 2k^2
- "Divide both sides of the equation 4k^2 = 2d^2 by 2 to get 2k^2 = d^2, which means that d^2 is also even."

8) Thus d^2 is even, so d is even.

9) Both n and d are divisible by 2, contradicting the assumption that they had no common factor greater than 1.
- "Since c and d are both even, 2 divides both n and d."
- "This contradicts the assumption that there is no integer greater than 1 that divides both n and d. Therefore, sqrt(2) must be irrational."


MAIN IDEA:
The proof doesn't simply say: "sqrt(2) is irrational because everyone know it."
It builds a chain:
ratiional assumption -> n even -> d even -> common factor -> contradiction.

Theorem: "Among any group of 25 people, the must be at least three who are all born in the same month."

Proof begins by assuming the opposite:
"There is a groupd of 25 people such thaat no three people have birthdays in the same month."


Step 1 - Define Variables
Let: x1, x2, ..., x12
- Represent the number of people born in each month.

Since everyone has a birthday in one of the 12 months:
x1 + x2 + ... + x12 = 25


Step 2 - Apply the contradiction assumption
- If no three people were born in the same month, then each month could have at most two people.

Thus: xi <= 2 for every month

So: x1 + x2 + ... + x12 <= 2 + 2 + ... + 2.
There are 12 months: 2(12) = 24.

Thus: 25 <= 24.
IMPOSSIBLE.

Step 3 - Contradiction
- We know there are 25 people, but the assumption says there can be at most 24.
Thuse the assumption is false.

Conclusion: There must be at least three people born in the same month.


Recognition Pattern:
- Assume something doesn't happen.
->
- Find the maximum possible amound.
->
- Show the required amount exceeds that maximum
->
- Contradiction

15. 5.5.7 - No Smallest Positive Real Number
Theorem: "There is no smallest positive real number." 

Step 1 - Assume the opposite
Assume: "There is a smallest positive real number called r."
As r is positive: r > 0
- "Since r is positive, r > 0. Dividing both sides of the inequality by 2 gives that r/2 > 0, which means that r/2 is also positive."


Step 2 - Create a smaller positive number
Divide by 2: r / 2 > 0
So: r / 2, is also positive
- "We can add r/2 to the inequality r/2 > 0 to get that r > r/2."

BUT: r / 2 < r.
THUS, we have found a positive real number smaller than r.
- "r/2 is a positive real number that is smaller than r."


Step 3 - Contradiction
- We assumed r was the smallest positive real number.
BUT: r / 2, is positive and smaller than r.
CONTRADICTION.
- "This contradicst the assumption that r is the smallest positive real number."

RESULT: "There is no smallest positive real number."

19. Common Mistakes in Proof by Contradiction

1) Proving the theorem directly.
- Don't begin by assuming the theorem.
Begin with: "The negation of the theorem."


2) Confusing the contradiction with contrapositive
Contrapositive: -q -> -p

Contradiction: p ∧ ¬q -> impossible result


3) Negating a statement incorrectly
EX: -(x < 0) = x >= 0
- NOT x > 0.


4) Forgetting Quantifiers
"For every..."
negates to:
"There exists..."

AND:
"There exists..."
negates to:
"For every..."


5) Only showing something surprising
- A contradiction must violate something known to be true.

EX: 25 <= 24, is impossible.
