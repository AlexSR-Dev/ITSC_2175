04 - 4.4 Modular Arithmetic

1. Arithmetic Modulo m
- For an integer m > 1, the mod operation gives the remainder after division by m.

x mod m
The result is always one of: 0,1,2,...,m-1

General steps
To calculate x mod m:
1) Divide x by m.
2) Find the remainder.
3) The remainder is x mod m.

Example: 12 mod 5 = 2
because: 12 = 5(2) + 2

Important:
- When working in Z_m, every result is reduced back into:
0 through m - 1

So:
3 + 4 = 7 = 2 (mod 5)
and
3 * 4 = 12 = 2 (mod 5)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


2. Addition and Multiplication Modulo m 
- Arithmetic in Z_M works like ordinary arithmetic, but reduce the result modulo m.

Addition:
(a + b) mod m

Steps:
1) Add the numbers.
2) Divide by m.
3) Keep the remainder.

EX:
6 + 8 = 14
14 mod 5 = 4

Thus: 6 + 8 = 4 (mod 5)


Multiplication:
(a * b) mod m

Steps:
1) Multiply.
2) Divide by m.
3) Keep the remainder.

EX:
6(8) = 48
48 mod 5 = 3

THUS: 6(8) = 3 (mod 5)

APPLICATION

3. Hash Functions
- Can use modular arithmetic to map a larger number to a smaller location in a hash table.

Common form: h(n) = cn mod T
Where:
- n = Input/ID number
- T = Size of the hash table
- c = constant

General Steps To calculate a hash value:
1. Identify n, c, and T.
2. Multiply c by n.
3. Calculate the result modulo T.
4. Use the resulting value as the table index.

The resulting index will be between: 0 and T - 1

4. Congruence Modulo m
- Two integers are congruent modulo m when they have the same remainder after division by m.

Notation: x == y (mod m)
Means: x mod m = y mod m

General method: determine whether two numbers are congruent
1) Calculate the remainder of the first number when divided by m.
2) Calculate the remainder of the second number when divided by m.
3) Compare the remainders.
4) If they are equal, the numbers are congruent.

EX:
To determine whether 17 and 5 are congruent modulo 12:

17 mod 12 = 5
5 mod 12 = 5

THUS: 17 == 5 (mod 12)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5. Precedence of the Mod Operation
- Mod is performed before addition/subtraction.

EX structure:
6 + 2 mod 7

First Calculate: 2 mod 7 = 2
Then: 6 + 2 = 8
So: 6 + 2 mod 7 = 8


With multiplication
- It occurs before mod when the product is the operand:
6 * 2 mod 7

First: 6 * 2 = 12
Then: 12 mod 7 = 5
Thus: 5

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6. Computing Arithmetic Expressions Modulo m
- You can reduce intermediate results modulo m instead of waiting until the very end.

Key properties are:
[(x mod m) + (y mod m)] mod m = (x + y) mod m

AND

[(x mod m)(y mod m)] mod m = (xy) mod  m

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7. General Strategy For Large Modular Expressions:

Step 1 - Identify the modulus (the num after the mod)
EX: (651^23 + 17) mod 10
m = 10

Step 2 - Reduce the components
EX: 
651 mod 10
AND
17 mod 10

Step 3 - Perform the Operation using the Reduced Values
Use:
(x + y) mod m
OR
(xy) mod m
when appropriate.

Step 4 - Reduce after each major operation
Keep intermediate results between: 0 and m - 1.
- To prevent large numbers.
