15 - 6.3 Direct graphs, paths, and cycles

1. Directed graphs (digraph)
- Is a pair G = (V, E), where V is a set of vertices and E is a set of directed edges, with E ⊆ V x V.

- A Directed edge (u,v) is an arrow from u to v.
: u is the tail; v is the head.
An edge whose head and tail are the same vertex is a self-loop

RECOGNIZE:
: (u,v) means follow the arrow from u -> v.
: In-degrees counts arrows entering a vertex.
: Out-degrees counts arrows leaving a vertex.
: A self-loop contributes to both in-degree and out-degree.


WHAT TO DO:
- For an ordered pair, check the arrow direction exactly.
- For degree questions, count incoming or outgoing directed edges rather than merely counting connected vertices.

NOTE:
(u,v) and (v,u) are different directed edges.

2. 6.3.1 - Directed graphs
- Interpret the displayed set V and edge set E, then identify tails, heads, and degrees.
TESTS: Directed-edge notation and in-degree/out-degree.

APPROACH:
- Reach each ordered pair as tail -> head, then count arrows entering/leaving the requested vertex.

STEP-BY-STEP:
1) For (a,b), a is the tail and b is the head.
2) For (b,c), b is the tail and c is the head.
3) For (d,d), the edge is a self-loop.
4) c has incoming edges from b and a, so in-degree(c) = 2.
5) b has one outgoing edge, so out-degree(b) = 1.
6) The self-loop at d contributes one to both in-degree(d) and out-degree(d).

THUS: in-degree(c) = 2; out-degree(b) = 1; in-degree(d) = 1 and out-degree(d) = 1.
WHY?
- Every result follows by the ordered pairs in E from its direction.

NOTE:
- Use the direction ofan edge.
Self-loop counts in both degree directions.

5. Walks in Directed Graphs
- Uses an analogy: airports are vertices and a directed edge from u to v represents a direct fligh from u to v.
A trip can be possible through a sequence of direct flights even without one direct flight.

RECOGNIZE:
- If a problem asks whether you can move from one vertex to another through a sequence of directed edges, look for a "walk".


A walk in a directed graph
- A walk from v to v is a sequence of alternating vertices and edges that starts and ends with a vertex.
Represented by its vertex sequence (v,v,...,v).

- Sequence is a walk only if (v,v) ∈ E for every transition.

LENGTH: Number of edges in the walk.
OPEN: First and last vertices differ.
CLOSED: First and last vertices are the same.

RECOGNIZE:
- Check every consecutive transition. Then count transitions for length.
Lastly compare the first and last vertices for open/closed status.

TRAP:
- A walk can repeat vertices or edges. Those repetitions matter only when testing stricter categories such as trails and paths.

6. 6.3.3 - Walks in Directed Graphs
ASKS: Determine the length and open/closed status of the example walks.
TESTS: Walk length and open/closed walks.

APPROACH: Trace the directed sequence, count its edges, then compare the endpoints.

STEP-BY-STEP:
1) The first displayed walk has four edges and different endpoints, so it is an open walk of length 4.
2) The second displayed walk was five edges and matching endpoints, so it is a closed walk of length 5.
3) Provides the closed walk (d,d) of length 1.
4) A single vertex (a) is a closed walk of length 0.

WHY?
- Length counts edges, not listed vertices.
Open/closed status depends only on the first and last vertices after the sequence is confirmed as a walk.

TRAP:

7. 6.3.4 - Identifying open and closed walks
Decision process:
(1) Verify every consecutive directed edge.
(2) If valid, compare the first and last vertices. Same -> closed; Diffferent -> open.

Q1)
Is (1,2,4) an open walk?
TESTS: Walk validity

APPROACH:
- Check 1 -> 2 and then 2 -> 4 before deciding open/closed.
STEP-BY-STEP:
1) 1 -> 2 exists.
2) 2 -> 4 does not exist.
3) THUS the sequence is not a walk.
THUS: FALSE


Q2)
Is (2,3,2) a closed walk?
TESTS: Closed-walk definition

APPORACH
- Verify 2 -> 3 and 3 -> 2, then compare endpoints.
STEP-BY-STEP
1) 2 -> 3 exists
2) 3 -> 2 exists
3) The sequence begins and ends at 2.

THUS: TRUE
WHY - It is a valid walk whose first and last vertices match.


Q3)
Is (1,2,3,2) an open walk?
TESTS: Open-walk definition

APPROACH:
- Verify each transition, then compare 1 and 2.
STEP-BY-STEP:
1) 1 -> 2 exists
2) 2 -> 3 exists
3) 3 -> 2 exists
4) The endpoints are different: 1 != 2

THUS: TRUE
WHY - All transitions are valid and the endpoints differ.


Q4)
Is (2,2,2) a closed walk?
TESTS: Self-loops and closed walks.

APPROACH:
- Use the self-loop 2 -> 2 for each transition, then compare endpoints.
STEP-BY-STEP:
1) 2 -> 2 is a self loop
2) The loop permits both transitions.
3) The sequence begins and ends at 2.

THUS: TRUE
WHY - A self-loop is a directed edge from a vertex to itself.

8. Trails, circuits, paths, and cycles
Trail - Is a walk in which no edge occurs more than once.
Path - Is a walk in which no vertex occurs more than once.
Circuit - Is a closed trail.
Cycle - Is a circuit of length at least 1 in which no vertex occurs more than once, except that the first and last vertices are the same.

RECOGNIZE:
First ask: Are edges repeated? -> trail test.
Then ask: Are vertices repeated? -> path/cycle test.
Finally ask: Is it closed? -> circuit/cycle test.

NOTE:
Trail may repeat a vertex; a path cannot.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9. 6.3.5 - Trails, Circuits, Paths, and Cycles
ASKS: Determine whether they are trails, paths, circuits, or cycles.
TESTS: Definitions of trail, path, circuit, and cycle.

APPROACH:
- Inspect edge repetition first, then vertex repetition, then whether the walk is closed.
STEP-BY-STEP:
1) (a,b,c,b,d) uses no edge more than once, so it is a trail.
2) Vertex b occurs twice, so that trail is not a path.
3) (b,d,c,b) is closed and uses no edge more than once, so it is a circuit.
4) Only the first/last vertex is repeated in (b,d,c,b), so it is also a cycle.

10. 6.3.6 Trails, circuits, paths, and cycles
1) (1,2,3,2,1)
- Circuit but not a cycle
WHY - Closed; no edge repeates, but vertex 2 repeats internally.

2) (2,1,3,4)
- Trail and path
WHY - No edge repeats and no vertex repeats.

3) (2,3,2)
- Circuit and cycle
WHY - Closed; directed edges are distinct, and only the endpoints repeat.

4) (1,4,3,1,2,5,1)
- Circuit but not a cycle
WHY - Closed; no edge repeats, but vertex 1 repeats internally.

TRAP:
In (2,3,2), 2 -> 3 and 3 -> 2 are different directed edges.
