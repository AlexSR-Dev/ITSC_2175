6.8 - Equivalence Relations

GOALS:
1. Determine if a relation is an equivalence relation by analyzing the properties of the relation.
2. Describe the partition of a set defined by an equivalence relation on that set.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


1. Equivalence Relation
- If the relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

Equivalence Relation = Reflexive + Symmetric + Transitive
- All three required.

1.1) Reflexive
- Every element is related to itself.
For a relation R on a set A:
: aRa for every a ∈ A

1.2) Symmetric
- If a is related to b, then b must be related to a.
: aRb -> bRa

1.3) Transitive
- If a is related to b, and b is related to c, then a must be related to c.
: aRb AND bRc -> aRc

Relation R on: A = {a,b,c,d,e,f}

APPROACH:

- Symmetric: Look for the reverse of every relationship between distinct elements.
- Transitive: Whenever there is a path x -> y -> z, verify that x -> z also exists.

3. Participation Activity 6.8.2 - Identifying Equivalence Relations
- Select the choice that applies to each relation. Each question gives a domain and a rule defining when xRy.

Q1) The domain of R is a group of employees at a company. xRx if x earns at least as much money as y. 
At least two employees at the company do not earn the same amount of money.

- xRy <--> x earns at least as much money as y.
- Determine whether the relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

APPROACH: Translate the relation into a mathematical comparison:
xRy <--> pay(x) >= pay(y)

- Step by Step:
1) Reflexive:
- Every employee earns at least as much as themselves.
- True, so the relation is reflexive.

2) Symmetric:
- Suppose employee x earns more than employee y. It does not follow that y earns at least as much as x.
EX: If x earns $60,000 and y earns $40,000, then xRy holds, but yRx does not.
- False, not symmetric.

3) Transitive:
- If x earns at least as much as y, and y earns at least as much as z, then x earns at least as much as z.
x >= y, y >= z  ->  x >= z
- True, the relation is transitive.

RESULT: R is not an equivalence relation because R is not symmetric.



Q2) The domain is the set of integers, with xRy <--> x^2 = y^2.
- Step by step:
1) Reflexive:
- For every integer x, x^2 = x^2
- Thus, R is reflexive.

2) Symmetric
- If x^2 = y^2, then y^2 = x^2, because equality is symmetric.

3) Transitive:
Suppose: x^2 = y^2, y^2 = z^2
- Since both squares equal y^2, x^2 = z^2
- Thus, R is transitive.

RESULT: R is an equivalence relation.



Q3) The domain of relation R is the set of integers greater than 1. xRy if a positive integer other than 1 evenly divides both x and y.
xRy <--> a positive integer other than 1 evenly divides both x and y.

- Step by Step:
1) Reflexive:
- Every integer x > 1 must share a permitted divisor with itself.
- Choose any x > 1. The number x itself is a positive integer greater 1, and divides x.
THUS: x shares a permitted divisor with itself, so the relation is reflexive.

2) Symmetric
- If a permitted positive integer divides both x and y, it also divides both y and x.
THUS: The relation is symmetric.

3) Transitive
- Suppose x and y share a permitted divisor, and y and z share a permitted divisor. Doesn't guarantee that x and z
share a permitted divisor.
EX: Let x = 2, y = 6, and z = 3
- 2 and 6 share divisor 2.
- 6 and 3 share divisor 3.
- 2 and 3 share no common positive divisor other than 1.
THUS: Fails

RESULT: R is not an equivalence relation because R is not transitive.

4. Equivalence Classes
- Suppose A is the domain of an equivalence relation and a ∈ A.
The equivalence class of a, denoted [a], is the set of all elements x ∈ A such that x ~ a.
[a] = {x ∈ A : x ~ a}

(x ~ a) - That x is equivalent to a under the relation.
OVERALL: An equivalence class groups together all elements that are equivalent to a chosen element.

What to Do:
1) Identify the element defining the class.
2) Apply the equivalence relation to every element in the domain.
3) Collect exactly the elements related to the chosen element.


EX: Remainders when divided by 3
- The domain is the natural numbers. Two natural numbers are related when they have the same remainder after division by 3.

Three equivalence classes:
[0] = {x ∈ N : x has remainder 0 when divided by 3}
[1] = {x ∈ N : x has remainder 1 when divided by 3}
[2] = {x ∈ N : x has remainder 2 when divided by 3}

- Groups natural numbers according to their remainders.

5. Participation Activity 6.8.3 - Equivalence Relations and Partitions
- The relation R on the set:
A = {a,b,c,d,e,f}
- The diagram identifies three equivalence classes:
{a,b,e}, {d,f}, {c}

- Finding equivalence classes from a relation diagram and recognizing the resulting partition.
APPROACH:
1) Identify which elements belong to the same equivalence class.
2) Write each class as a set.
3) Check that every element appears.
4) Ensure no element appears in two distinct classes.
5) Remove duplicate representations of the same class.

- Step by Step
Class 1: The diagram groups a,b,e together.
[a] = [b] = [e] = {a,b,e}
Class 2: The diagram groups d,f together.
[d] = [f] = {d,f}
Class 3: The element c forms its own class.
[c] = {c}

The classes cover all six elements:
{a,b,e} U {d,f} U {c} = A
- They are also pairwuise disjoint.

RESULT: Partition is: {{a,b,e}, {d,f}, {c}}
- Every element of A belongs to exactly one of the three equivalence classes.

6. Participation Activity 6.8.4 - Equivalence Relations and Partitions
A = {a,b,c,d,e}
And the relation:
R = {{a,d}, (a,e), (a,a), (b,b), (b,c), (c,c), (c,e), (a,b), (d,d), (e,e)}

- What is the partition defined by the equivalence relation R?

ASKS: Determine which elements belong to each equivalence class, then identify the collection of distinct classes.
APPROACH:
For each element, identify all elements equivalent to it. Because the relation is stated to be an equivalence relation, equivalent 
elements belong to the same class.


- Step by Step:
1. Group a, e
- The relation contains (a,a) and (a,e), meaning a is related to itself and to e.
[a] = [e] = {a,e}


2. Group b
- The relation contains (b,b) nut no other pairs involving b.
[b] = {b}

3. Group c,d
- The relation contains (c,c) and (c,d), meaning c is related to itself and to d.
[c] = [d] = {c,d}
 
3. Verify Coverage.
- The two class contains every element of A:
{a,e} U {b} U {c,d} = A.

- They are disjoint, and each element belongs to exactly one class.

RESULT: {a,e} U {b} U {c,d}
