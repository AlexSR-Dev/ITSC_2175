10.3 - Counting Permutations

OBJ: Using permutations and the generalized product rule to count ordered arrangements of objects selected from a finite set.
GOALS: 
- Use r-permutations to count the number of possible outcomes in a given scenario.
- Count the number of permutations for a given set.
- Use the product rule in conjunction with principles of permutations to count the number of elements in a set.

2. Understanding Permutations
- Is an ordered arrangement of elements.

SET: X = {John, Paul, George, Ringo}
CONSIDER: Two possible sequences containing three memebers of this set:
(Pau, Ringo, John), (John, George, Paul)
- Both are examples of 3-permutations of X.
NOTE: Those two sequences are not equals to each other, AS ORDER MATTERS.


2.2) What is an r-permutation?
- Is a sequence of 'r' items selected from the same set, with no repetition.

FIFTH: Position

8 * 7 * 6 * 5 * 4 = 6,720

3. The Number of r-Permutations from a Set with 'n' elements
- Let 'r' and 'n' be positive integers such that: r <= n

- The number of r-permutations from a set with 'n' elements is denoted by: P(n,r)
FORMULA: P(n,r) = n! / (n-r)!

3.1) Factorial Notation
'factorial' - Is the product of all positive integers from a given number down to 1.
EX: 5! = 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120

- r-permutations uses factorials to represent the decreasing number of choices.


3.2) Why the Formula Works
START: Generalized Product Rule
: For a set containing 'n' elements:
- The first position has n choices.
- The second position has n-1 choices.
- The third position has n-2 choices.
- This continues until r elements have been selected.

THUS: P(n,r) = n(n-1)(n-2)...(n-r+1)

Expanded Form Permutation Formula:
n! = n(n-1)(n-2)...(n-r+1)(n-r)!
- Divide both sides by (n-r)!
P(n,r) = n! / (n-r)!


3.3) Apply the Formula
- The previous activity involved selecting 5 elements from a set of 8.
THUS: n = 8, r = 5

SUB:
P(8,5) = 8! / (8 - 5)!
= 8! / 3!
NOTE: 3! cancels the 3! from 8!, thus ends at 3 instead of continuing donw.
= 8 * 7 * 6 * 5 * 4
= 6,720

RESULT: The same as generalized product rule.

4. Participation Activity 10.3.2 - Counting r-Permutations
- Each question can be solved with generalized product rule or the r-permutation formula.

5.1) Three dices are thrown: one red, one blue, and one green
- Each die has six possible outcomes.

Q1) How many outcomes arwe possible in which all three dice show different numbers?
STEP 1: Select the red die's result
- The red die can show any number from 1-6.
- 6 choices

STEP 2: Select the blue die's result
- Blue die must show a different number from the red die.
- 5 choices

STEP 3: Select the green die's result
- Must be different from the previous dices.
- 4 choices

APPLY: Generalized Product Rule:
6 * 5 * 4 = 120

APPLY: 3-permutation
P(6,3) = 6!/3! = 120


Q2) How many ways can the students be seated at the computers if no computer has more than one student and each student is seated at a 
computer?
NOTE: 5 computers and three students.
STEP 1: Seat the first student
- 5 available computers.
- 5 choices

STEP 2: Seat the second student
- Once computer is already occupied.
- 4 choices

STEP 3: Seat the third student
- 3 choices

APPLY: Generalized Product Rule:
5 * 4 * 3 = 60

APPLY: 3-permutation
P(5,3) = 5!/(5 - 3)! = 60

5. Counting Permutations of an Entire Set
- Arranging every element in a finite set exactly once.

5.1) Definition - A permutation without the parameter 'r'
'permutation' - Of a finite set is a sequence that contains every element of the set exactly once.

EX: A = {a,b,c}
- A permutation must contain all three elements, without repetition.

5.2) The Number of Permutations of a Finite Set
P(n,n) = n(n-1) ... 2 * 1 = n!

: This formula follows directly from the generalized product rule.
- The first position has n choices.
- The second position has n-1 choices.
- The third position has n-2 choices.
- This continues until every element has been selected.

5.3) EX - Counting Permutations of Three Elements
SET: A = {a,b,c}
- There are three choices for first, 2 for second, and 1 for third.
3! = 3 * 2 * 1 = 6

PROVIDES: A wedding party consisting of:

• Two groomsmen
- All six people must line up for a photograph.

Q) How many ways can the wedding party line up?
STEP 1: Identify the number of people
1 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 6 people.

STEP 2: Determine whether order matters
- Must be arranged in a line.
THUS: Is a full permutation of six distinct individuals.

STEP 3: Apply the permutation formula
6! = 6 * 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 720

7. Combining the Product Rule with Permutation Counting
- Instead of arranging everyone freely, two specific people must sit next to each oother.

SET: X = {John, Paul, George, Ringo}
Q) How many possible seating arrangements are there if Paul and John must sit next to each other?

8.1) Identify the restriction
- Paul and John must be adjacent. Thus must appear together in either order:
(John, Paul) OR (Paul, John)


8.2) Treat the two people as one block [John + Paul]
- The object being arranged are now:
1. George
2. Ringo
3. The combinded John-Paul block.

NOTE: There are three objects to arrange.
ARRAGEMENT TOTAL: 3! = 3 * 2 * 1 = 6


8.3) Account for the two possible internal orders
- Since John and Paul are treated as one block, they can still appear in two differen orders (mentioned in 8.1).
THUS: Block as two internal arrangements.

APPLY: Product Rule:
3! * 2 = 6 * 2 = 12

8. Participation Activity 10.3.5 - Examples of Counting Subsets
SCENARIO: The wedding party consist of:
• One bride
• One groom
• Two bridesmaids
• Two groomsmen
- Two people in total.

Q) How many ways can the wedding party line up so taht the bride is next to the groom?
STEP 1 - Treat the bride and groom as one block
COMBINE: [Bride + Groom]
REMAINING PEOPLE: 2 bridesmaids and 2 groomsmen.
THUS: 1 block + 4 individual = 5 objects

STEP 2 - Arrange the five objects
5! = 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120

STEP 3 - Account for the internal order of the block
- Bride and Grrom can appear in two possible values: (bride, groom) OR (groom, bride)
THUS: 2 arragements.

STEP 4 - Apply the Product Rule
5! * 2 = 240
