10.2 - The Generalized Product Rule

GOAL: Applying the generalized product rule to count the number of possible outcomes when a process involves multiple sequential choices.

EXPLORES:
1. A race with first-, second-, and third-place winners.
2. A family arranging themselves in a single-file hiking order.
3. Employees selecting offices and desks.
4. A club electing officers without allowing anyone to hold more than one position.

2. The Generalized Product Rule

2.1) Understanding sequential choices
- A race involving 20 runners.

- There are three trophies:
• First place
• Second place
• Third place
- Outcome is determined by identifying who wins each trophy.

Q) How many different outcomes are possible?
NOTE: Condition is that a runner cannot win more than one of the three trophoes.
THUS: The number of available choices changes after each winner is selected.

STEP 1: Select the first-place winner.
- All 20 runners are eligible.

STEP 2: Select the second-place winner.
- One runner already won first place, so that runner cannot win second place.
20 - 1 = 19 choices.

STEP 3: Select the third-place winner.
- Two runners has already been selected
20 - 2 = 18 choices.

APPLY: The Product Rule:
20 * 19 * 18 = 6,840


2.2) Why this is a Generalized product-rule problem?
- The race illustrates that counting can be seen as a sequence of decisions.
- Each step, another item is selected from the set of items being counted.

OBSERVATION:
• The first selection has 20 possibilities.
• The second selection has 19 possibilities.
• The third selection has 18 possibilities.
• Each selection contributes another factor to the total.

THUS: Number of choices decrease from previous selections that cannot be selected again.

Look for CONDITIONS:
• No person or item can be selected more than once.
• Several positions must be filled.
• The order of selection matters.
• Each decision reduces the number of remaining possibilities.


2.3) Definition - Generalized Product Rule
CONSIDER: A set 'S' of sequence of 'k' items.

SUPPOSE:
- n_1 choices are available for the first item.
- For every possible choice of the first item, n_2 choices are available for the second item.
- For every possible choice of the first and second items, n_3 choices are available for the third item.
- This pattern continues.
- For every possible choice of the first k-1 items, n_k choices are available for the k-th item.


THEN: |S| = n_1 * n_2 * n_3 ... n_k
                        k
PRODUCT NOTATION: |S| = II n_i
                        i=1


NOTE: n_i represents the number of available choices at a particular step.

3. Participation Activity 10.2.1 - An Example of the Generalized Product Rule
ASKS: A family of four is going on a hiking trip.
• Two parents: Mom & Dad
• Two childrend: Sister and Brother

- The trail is narrow, and must be walked in a single file.
SEQUENCE: (Parent, Child, Child, Parent)

Q) How many ways can the family walk in this order?
3.1) Identify the choices at each position
STEP 1: Select the first person (Parent).
- There are two parents.
THUS: 2 choices

STEP 2: Select the second person (Child).
- There are two children.
THUS: 2 choices

STEP 3: Select the third person (Child).
- One child has already been selected.
THUS: 1 choice.

STEP 4: Select the fourth person (Parent).
- One parent has alreadt been selected.
THUS: 1 choice

APPLY: Generalized Product Rule:
2 * 2 * 1 * 1 = 4

4. Example 10.2.1 - Counting Office and Desk Selection by the Generalized Product Rule

4.1) Scenario
- Three employees work in a startup.
• Eight Different Offices.
• Four Different Desks.
- Each employee selects one office and one desk.

Q) How many ways can the selection be done?

4.2) Count the founder's choices
• 8 available offices
• 4 available desks

APPLY: Product Rule
8 * 4 = 32 possible office-desk combinations.

4.3) Count the second employee's choices
- after the founder's selection, two items are no longer available.
- Second employee has:
• 7 remaining offices
• 3 remaining desks
THUS: 7 * 3 = 21

4.4) Count the third employee's choices
- Again one less items.
THUS: 6 * 2 = 12

4.5) Apply the Generalized Product Rule
(8 * 4) * (7 * 3) * (6 * 2) = 32 * 21 * 12 = 8,064

5. Participation Activity 10.2.2 - Examples of Counting Subsets
- Apply the Generalized Product Rule to selecting officers for a club.

5.1) Scenario
- A club has 10 studnets.
- Must elect four officers:
a) President.
b) Vice President.
c) Secretary
d) Treasurer.

NOTE: No student can hold more than one position.


Q1) How manny ways are there to select the class officers?
STEP 1: Select the President.
- 10 choices

STEP 2: Select the vice president.
- 9 choices

STEP 3: Select the secretary.
- 8 choices

STEP 4: Select the treasurer.
- 7 choices

APPLY: The Generalized Product Rule:
10 * 9 * 8 * 7 = 5,040


Q2) How many ways can the officiers be elected if the president is a tenth grader?
Club has:
• Five ninth graders
• Five tenth graders
RESTRICTION: President must be a tenth grader.
STEP 1: Select the president.
- Only 5 tenth graders are eligible.
- 5 choices

STEP 2: Select the vice president.
- 9 choices, as one student overall has been choosen.

STEP 3: Select the secretary
- 8 choices

STEP 4: Select the treasurer
- 7 choices

APPLY: The Generalized Product Rule:
5 * 9 * 8 * 7 = 2,520


Q3) How many ways can the officers be elected if the president is a tenth grader and the vice president is a ninth grader?
• Five ninth graders
• Five tenth graders
RESTRICTIONS: president MUST be a tenth grader AND vice president MUST be a ninth grader.
STEP 1: Select the president.
- Only 5 tenth graders.
- 5 choices

STEP 2: Select the vice president.
- Only 5 ninth graders.
- 5 choices

STEP 3: Select the secretary.
- Two students has already been choosen from the total 10.
- 8 choices

STEP 4: Select the treasurer.
- 7 choices

APPLY: The Generalized Product Rule:
5 * 5 * 8 * 7 = 1,400
