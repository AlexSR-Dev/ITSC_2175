10.6 - Counting by Complement

OBJ: Use the principle of counting by complement to count the number of elements in a set.

IDEA: Sometimes it is easier to count what you do not want and subtract it from the total than to count what you do want directly.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


2. Counting by Complement
SUPPOSE: A room contains:
• 20 people in total.
• Exactly 12 people do not have red hair.

DETERMINE: How many people have red hair, subtract the number without red hair from the total:
20 - 12 = 8
THUS: 8 people have red hair.

'Complement' - Is the set of elements that do not have the property being counted.


SUPPOSE:
• S is the entire set of elements.
• P is the subset of elements in S that have a particular property.
• /P is the complement of P, containing the elements that do not have that property.
• |S| - Total number of elements.
• |P| - Number of elements with property.
• |/P| - Number of elements without property.

COUNTING PRINCIPLE: |P| = |S| - |/P|


RECOGNIZE:
• "At least one"
• "At least one occurrence"
• "One or more"
• "Does not have the property"
• "None of the elements have the property"
- These indicate that counting the opposite situation is easier.

WHAT TO DO:
1) Identify the total set.
2) Identify the desired property.
3) Determine the complement: the elements that do not have that property.
4) Count the complement.
5) Subtract the complement from the total.

Desired Count = Total Count - Complement Count

3. Participation Activity 10.6.1 - Counting by Complement
Q) The number of 8-bit strings with at least one 0.

STEP 1: Identify the total set
- An 8-bit string contains 8 positions, each position contains either:
• 0
• 1
MULTIPLICATION PRINCIPLE: |S| = 2^8 = 256 possible 8-bit strings.


STEP 2: Identify the desired property
- We WANT to count the 8-bit strings that have at least one 0.
THUS: Rather than listing every string with 0, consider the opposite situation.


STEP 3: Identify the complement
COMPLEMENT - All 8-bit strings that have no 0s.
THUS: Only one such string: 1111 1111
RESULT: |/P| = 1


STEP 4: Apply Counting by Complement
FORMULA: |P| = |S| = |/P|
SUB: |P| = 256 - 1
RESULT: |P| = 255

4. Participation Activity 10.6.2 - Selecting Permutations or Subsets
RECOGNIZE: The appropriate counting method.
- When selecting people for a group, the order of selection generally does not matter.This is a "combination", or subset-counting problem.
COMBINATION FORMULA:
(n r) = n! / r!(n-r)!
• 'n' - The total number of available elements.
• 'r' - The number of elements selected.


- When arranging people in a particular order, the order matters. This is a "permutation" problem.
PERMUTATION FORMULA:
n!
• 'n' - Distinct elements.


Q1) Selecting two competitors from ten kids.
Q) There are ten kids on the math team. Two kids are selected from the team to compete in the state competition. How many ways are there 
to select the two competitors?

STEP 1: Identify what is being counted.
• Total available kids: n = 10.
• Number selected: r = 2.
• The order of selection does not matter.


STEP 2: Apply the combination formula
(10 2) = 10! / 2!(10-2)!
= 10! / 2!8!
= 10 * 9 / 2 * 1
RESULT: 45



Q2) Selecting two girsl from the math team.
Q) The math team has six girls and four boys. How many ways can the two competitors be selected if they are both girls?

STEP 1: Identify the eligible group
RESTRICTION: Selects ONLY girls.
• Eligible girls: n = 6.
• Number selected: r = 2.


STEP 2: Apply the combination formula
(6 2) = 6! / 2!(6-2)!
= 6! / 2!4!
= 6 * 5 / 2 * 1
RESULT: 15



Q3) Selecting two competitors so that at least one boy is chosen
Q) The math team has six girls and four boys. How many ways can the two competitors be chosen so that at least one boy is chosen?
NOTE: This combines combinations with counting by complement.

STEP 1: Identify the desired condition
RESTRICTION: Two competitors, with at least one boy.
TWO POSSIBILITIES:
• One boy & one girl.
• Two boys
- Counting by complement is simpler.


STEP 2: Identify the complement
- Opposite of selecting at least one boy is selecting no boys.
THUS: Selecting no boys means selecting two girls.
(6 2) = 15 (FROM Question 2)
THUS: 15 selections DO NOT satisfy the desired condition.


STEP 3: Count the total possible selections
- Ten team members, and select two:
(10 2) = 45


STEP 4: Apply counting by complement
At least one boy = Total Selections - No Boy Selections
= 45 - 15
RESULT: 30


ANOTHER METHOD:
- Separate cases by counting the two possible cases directly:
- One boy and one girl:
(4 1) * (6 1) = 4(6) = 24

- Two boys:
(4 2) = 6

- Add the possibilites:
24 + 6 = 30



Q4) Arranging four people so John is not next to Paul.
Q) Four people (John, Paul, George, and Ringo) are seated in a row on a bench. The number of ways to order the four people so that John 
is next to Paul is 12. How many ways can the four people be ordered on the bench so that John is not next to Paul?

STEP 1: Identify the total number of arrangements:
- Four distinct people:
• John
• Paul
• George
• Ringo

- USE PERMUTATIONS since people are arranged in a row thus ORDER MATTERS:
4! = 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 24 total arrangements.


STEP 2: Identify the complement
DESIRED CONDITION: John is not next to Paul
COMPLEMENT: John is next to Paul.
|/P| = 12


STEP 3: Apply Counting by Complement
|P| = |S| - |/P|
= 24 - 12
RESULT: 12

5. Challenge Activity - 10.6.1: Counting Possibilities by Complement

Q1) A pet shelter has 10 different dogs and 8 different cats.
ASKS: How many ways are there to select 4 pets to adopt?
STEP 1: Identify
TOTAL: n = 18
SELECTED: r = 4


STEP 2: Apply the combination formula
(18 4) = 18! / 4!(18-4)!
= 18! / 4!14!
RESULT: 3060



Q2) An auto dealer has 11 different cars and 7 different trucks in stock.
ASKS: How many ways are there to select 6 cars to purchase?
STEP 1: Identify
TOTAL: n = 11, since it is not trucks.
SELECTED: r = 6

STEP 2: Apply combination formula
(11 6) = 462
RESULT: 462



Q3) A bicycle shop has 10 different mountain bikes and 8 different road bikes in stock.
ASKS: How many ways are there to select 3 bikes so that at least one is a mountain bike?
STEP 1: Identify
RESTRICTION: One mountain bike

STEP 2: Idenfity the Complement
- Opposite of selecting a mountain bike is none at all.
THUS: Selecting just road bikes:
(8 3) = 56
THUS: 56 selections DO NOT SATISFY the CONDITION.

STEP 3: Count total possible Selections:
(18 3) = 816

STEP 4: Apply Counting by Complement
At least one mountain bike = Total Selection - No Mountain Bike
= 816 - 56 = 760
RESULT = 760



Q4) A pet shelter has 5 different dogs and 7 different cats.
ASKS: How many ways are there to select 4 pets so that at least one is a cat?
STEP 1: Identify
CONDITION: One cat

STEP 2: Identify the Complement
- Opposite of selecting a cat is none at all.
THUS: Selecting just dogs:
(5 4) = 5

STEP 3: Count total sections:
(12 4) = 495

STEP 4: Apply Counting by Complement
At least one cat = Total Selection - No cat
= 495 - 5 = 490
RESULT: 490



Q5) A password consists of 6 characters that contain a digit [0-9], an upper case letter, or one of the special characters from
[&,S,#,%].
ASKS: How many passwords are possible if the password must contain at least one digit?
STEP 1: Identify
CONDITION: One digit

STEP 2: Identify the Complement
- Opposite of selecting a digit is none at all.
THUS: Selecting everything else:
30^6

STEP 3: Count total sections:
40^6

STEP 4: Apply Counting by Complement
At least one digit = Total Selection - No digit
= 40^6 - 30^6
RESULT: 40^6 - 30^6

ASKS: How many strings of length 7 are possible if the string must contain at least one digit and one letter?
