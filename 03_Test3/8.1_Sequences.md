1. Sequence Fundamentals
• Sequence - Is a special type of function in which the domain is a set of consecutive integers.

EX: A student's GPA represented by a sequence over four years.
DOMAIN: {1,2,3,4}
g(1) = 3.67,    g(2) = 2.88
g(3) = 3.25,    g(4) = 3.75

DEFS & NOTATIONS:
a_k   
- The "term" of the sequence at index k.

k     
- The "index" identifying a term's position.

{a_k} 
- The entire sequence, including its terms.

a_k w/o braces 
- One individual term, not the entire sequence.

Intitial index m 
- The index where the sequence begins.

Final index n 
- The index where a finite sequence ends.

Initial term a_m 
- The first term of the sequence.

Final term a_n 
- The last term of a finite sequence.



1.1) Finite Versus Infinite Sequences
Finite - A sequence with a finite domain, has an initial index m and a final index n, where n >= m.
a_m, a_m + 1, ..., a_n

Infinite - A sequence with an infinite domain. Its indices continues indefinitely in the positive direction.
Has an initial index m, and the sequence is defined for every integer k >= m.
a_m, a_m + 1, a_m + 2, ...


1.2) Explicit Formula
- Specifies a term directly in terms of its index k.
EX: d_k = 2^k, k >= 1

- Substitute the desired index to calulate a term:
d_1 = 2^1 = 2, d_2 = 2^2 = 4
- Thus the sequence begins:
2,4,8,16,...

NOTE: Based on where the sequence starts like index k = 2, means that term would be the start and the third term k = 3 would be the 
second term.

4) Increasing and Decreasing Sequences
• Increasing
: a_k < a_(k+1)

• Nondecreasing
: a_k <= a_(k+1)

• Decreasing
: a_k > a_(k+1)

• Nonincreasing
: a_k >= a(k+1)


NOTE:
- An increasing sequence is always nondecreasing.
- A decreasing sequence is always nonincreasing.
- The vice versa AREN'T guaranteed, due to the strict inequality.

EX:
2 < 4 < 5 < 6
- This sequence is increasing and nondecreasing

2 <= 4 <= 4 <= 6
- Now the sequence is nondecreasing but not increasing.

6 > 5 > 4 > 2
- Decreasing and nonincreasing.

6 >= 4 >= 4 >= 2
- Nonincreasing but not decreasing.

6) Geometric Sequences
- A sequence of real numbers in which each term after the inital is found by taking the previous term
and multiplying by a fixed number called the common ratio.
NOTE: Can be finite or infinite.

DEFS & NOTATIONS:
a - Initial term, when initial index is 0.
r - common ratio
a_k - term at index k
a_(k+1) = r a_k - Recurrence relationship between consecutive terms.

- If the initial index is 0, the explicit formula is:
a_k = a*r^k, k >= 0


EX: Common Ratio
CONSIDER: 4,2,1,1/2,...
- Divide each term by the previous term:
r = 2/4 = 1/2
1/2 = 1/2
- The sequence is geometric with the initial term 4 and common ratio 1/2.

NOTE:
- A geometric sequence will be increasing if r > 1
- decreasing if 0 < r < 1.
- When the initial term a is positive.

7) Participation Activity 8.1.5 - Geometric Sequences
Q1) Let {s_n} be a geometric that starts with an initial index of 0.
The initial term is 2, and the common ratio is 5. What is s_2.

: s_k = 2(5)^k
: s_2 = 2(5)^2 = 2(25) = 50
OR
: s_0 = 2
: s_1 = s_0 * 5 = 10
: s_2 = s_1 * 5 = 50

s_k = 16(1/2)^k
s_3 = 16(1/2)^3 = 16(1/8) = 2

What is the common ratio.
r = 6/3 = 2

8) Arithmetic Sequences
- Is a sequence of real numbers in which each term after the initial term is found by taking the previous term and
adding a fixed number called the common difference.
NOTE: Can be finite or infinite.

DEFS & NOTATIONS:
a_0 - Initial term when the sequence starts at index 0.
d - Common difference
a_(k+1) = a_k + d - Recurrence relationship between consecutive terms.

- If the initial index is 0, the explicit formula is:
a_k = a_0 + dk, k >= 0

- Common difference:
d = a_(k+1) - a_k


EX: Common Difference
CONSIDER: -1,1.5,4,6.5,...
- Subtract consecutive terms:
1.5 -(-1) = 2.5
4 - 1.5 = 2.5
6.5 - 4 = 2.5
COMMON DIFFERENCE: d = 2.5


INFINITE Arithmetic Sequence:
- Initial term a_0 = 2 and common difference d = 3:
2,5,8,11,...

EXPLICIT FORMULA:
a_n = 2 + 3n, n >= 0


FINITE Arithmetic Sequence:
- Initial term a_0 = 3, final index 5, and common difference d = -2:
3,1,-1,-3,-5,-7

9) 8.1.7 - Arithmetic Sequences

Q1) Let {s_n} be a arithmetic sequence that starts with an initial index of 0.
The initial term is 3, and the common difference is -2. What is s_2.
: s_k = 3 - 2k
: s_2 = 3 - 2(2) = 3 - 4 = -1
OR
: s_0 = 3
: s_1 = s_0 + (-2) = 1
: s_2 = s_1 + (-2) = -1

Q2) Consider the geometric sequence: 7,4,1,...
What is the next term in the sequence?

COMMON DIFFERENCE:
d = 4 - 7 = -3
CONTINUE by Adding -3:
1 + (-3) = -2
